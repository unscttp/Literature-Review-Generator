# Writing a review

A review tells the reader what a body of work shows, not what each paper
says. Each paragraph makes a point and backs it with several papers.

## Steps

1. **Structure.** Follow `references/structure.md`: introduction, method,
   one section per theme, discussion, conclusion, and the abstract last.
2. **Paragraphs.** Start with the point (a topic sentence), then the
   evidence from the papers, then what it means for the research question.
   Read `references/style.md`.
3. **Citations.** Cite every statement about a paper with its library id,
   `[P12]`, or several, `[P3, P7, P9]`. Do not write author names with years:
   the app formats the citations and the reference list in the chosen style.
   You may name an author in the sentence ("Smith [P4] argues ...") when the
   person matters.
4. **Claims.** Report what papers claim as their claims ("[P4] reports ...",
   "studies with younger learners find ... [P2, P8]"). Your synthesis
   belongs in topic sentences, comparisons and the discussion. Hedge where
   the evidence is thin.
5. **Honesty.** Say what the review did not cover: languages, sources,
   years, papers without abstracts.

For a review in Simplified Chinese also read `references/chinese-academic.md`.
