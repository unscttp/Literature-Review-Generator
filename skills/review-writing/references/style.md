# Style

- **Synthesis over summary.** Not "P1 studied X. P2 studied Y." but "Studies
  of X agree that ... [P1, P2, P5], although those with older learners find
  ... [P7]."
- **Topic sentences** carry the argument; a reader skimming only first
  sentences should follow the review.
- **Signal relations:** similarly, in contrast, building on, unlike, extends,
  challenges.
- **Precise verbs** for what papers do: argues, shows, reports, finds,
  proposes, questions, replicates.
- **Hedging** matches the evidence: "suggests" for one small study, "shows"
  for consistent findings across several.
- **Paragraphs** of 120-200 words; one point each.
- **Avoid** lists of papers, long quotations, evaluating papers only by their
  citation count, and claims without a citation.
- **Markdown:** paragraphs, `###` subheadings inside a section, tables for
  comparisons where they help. No top-level `#` or `##` headings: the section
  title is added automatically.
