# Sections of a literature review

| Section | Content | Length (share) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract | Aim, sources, number of included papers, main findings, gaps | 150-250 words |
| Introduction | Context and why the topic matters, aims, research questions, how the review is organized | 10% |
| Method | Sources searched, queries, years, criteria, numbers found, screened, included, with the main reasons for exclusion | 10% |
| Theme sections | One per theme or group of themes; compare and connect the papers | 55-65% |
| Discussion | Synthesis across themes, contradictions and their reasons, gaps, directions for research | 15% |
| Conclusion | Answers to the research questions in a few paragraphs | 5% |

Section ids are short and stable: `introduction`, `method`, `theme-...`,
`discussion`, `conclusion`, `abstract`.

The method section of a narrative review is short but real: name the
databases (OpenAlex, arXiv), the queries, the dates and the counts. Do not
call the review "systematic" unless the request's review_type is systematic,
and even then say that screening was done by an AI model with human approval
of the scope only.
