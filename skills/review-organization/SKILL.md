# Organizing a review

A review is organized around ideas, not papers. The structure should answer
the research questions: a reader should see what is known, how the work
relates and what is open.

## Steps

1. **Choose the principle.** Read `references/structures.md`. Themes suit
   most reviews; methods suit fields where the approach decides the result;
   chronology suits fields whose questions changed over time; theories or
   debates suit fields organized around positions.
2. **Group.** Build 3-7 groups. Each included paper belongs to one to three
   (`library_tag`). A group with one paper is a note, not a group; a group
   with half the papers needs splitting.
3. **Compare within groups.** What do the papers share, where do they
   disagree, and why (different data, methods, periods, definitions)?
4. **Compare across groups.** What connects the groups, which questions
   does none of them answer?
5. **Gaps.** Distinguish gaps in the literature (nobody studied X) from gaps
   in this review (the search did not find X).

Read `references/synthesis.md` for comparison techniques.
